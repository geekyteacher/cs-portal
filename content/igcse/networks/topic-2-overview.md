## What this topic covers

Topic 2 has three syllabus sections. Work through them in order; the pages
combine revision notes with class activities, practice, and short homework.

| Syllabus section | Focus | Pages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2.1 Types and methods of data transmission** | Packets and packet switching; serial and parallel; simplex, half-duplex, and full-duplex; USB. | [Packets and packet switching](./data-transmission), [Transmission methods](./transmission-methods), [USB](./usb) |
| **2.2 Methods of error detection** | Parity checks and blocks, checksums, check digits, and automatic repeat request (ARQ). | [Error detection](./error-detection) |
| **2.3 Encryption** | Plaintext and ciphertext; symmetric and asymmetric encryption; public and private keys. | [Encryption](./encryption) |

## Suggested route through the topic

Use this as a flexible three-week route for three 45-minute periods each
week. The double period gives time for explanations and activities; the
single period is useful for focused practice and a checkpoint. Adjust the
pace if students need more time.

| Week | Double period | Single period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | 2.1: packets, packet switching, and transmission methods. Use a packet-routing or packet-reassembly activity, then compare serial/parallel and direction methods. | Finish 2.1 transmission-method practice; introduce USB and assign the first short homework task. |
| **2** | Finish 2.1 with USB scenarios and an exam-style checkpoint. Begin 2.2 with parity bits and parity blocks. | Continue 2.2 with checksums, check digits, and ARQ; practise describing how an error is detected and what happens next. |
| **3** | Consolidate 2.2 through worked examples and exam questions; address any gaps shown by the checkpoint. | Teach 2.3 encryption and finish with a short mixed-topic retrieval check. |

The site pages are organised by syllabus section, not by individual
45-minute lesson. A page may support more than one class period.

## How to use the pages

1. Read the explanation and worked examples for the section being taught.
2. Complete the class practice or the matching activity worksheet your
   teacher assigns.
3. Finish the short homework at the end of the page and check corrections
   against the teacher-provided mark scheme.
4. Use the exit checks for retrieval before moving to the next section.

The printable activity worksheets and their mark schemes are separate from
these notes. Follow your teacher's instructions about which worksheet to
complete and when to use its answers.

## Topic checklist

- [ ] Describe packet structure and explain packet switching.
- [ ] Compare serial and parallel transmission and explain simplex,
  half-duplex, and full-duplex.
- [ ] Explain the benefits and drawbacks of USB.
- [ ] Explain and apply the error-detection methods in section 2.2.
- [ ] Explain symmetric and asymmetric encryption and the roles of the keys.
- [ ] Answer exam-style questions using accurate technical terms and
  scenario-based reasons.
