## Today you will

Showcase your game, explain how one part of its code works, and evaluate
what you would improve next.

## Prepare your game

Before sharing, check that:

- the game starts in the correct state;
- the player controls work;
- interactions update the game as intended;
- the win or game-over ending is clear;
- the game can be restarted; and
- the project has been saved.

Prepare a short explanation of your game title, player action, challenge,
and goal.

## Showcase and play

Take turns playing classmates' games. For each game, notice one feature
that works well and one thing you would like to know about how it was
built. Leave constructive feedback.

When presenting your own game, demonstrate one mechanic and explain the
blocks that make it work. You could explain:

- how the player moves;
- how a sprite detects a collision;
- how a variable tracks progress; or
- how a custom block checks the game state.

## Evaluate using evidence

Complete a short evaluation:

1. What were your two success criteria?
2. Which one did you meet most successfully? What test, screenshot, or
   demonstration supports your answer?
3. What bug or playtest suggestion did you act on?
4. What changed after you tested the fix?
5. If you had one more lesson, what would you improve next?

Be specific. “I fixed the hazard” is less useful than “I added a wait
until the sprites were no longer touching, so one collision no longer
removed several lives.”

## Final evidence checklist

- [ ] My game plan or prototype from Lesson 1.
- [ ] A record of my controls, interaction, and challenge scripts (screenshots,
  sketches, or descriptions).
- [ ] A record of my custom block and game ending.
- [ ] My test table and peer feedback.
- [ ] One recorded change and the result of testing it again.
- [ ] My final evaluation.

## Optional next steps

If your core game is working and your evidence is complete, choose one
extension:

- add another level or difficulty setting;
- add a high-score or best-time variable;
- improve the instructions, sound, or visual feedback;
- add another hazard with a different behaviour; or
- use a custom block to organise another repeated task.

Add and test one feature at a time.

## Hand in your work

Save or submit your Scratch project using your teacher's instructions. If
your class has a submission method, provide the project link or upload the
file as directed. Include your project log or evidence document if your
teacher has asked for it. If uploading is not practical, be ready to open
and demonstrate your saved project in class.
